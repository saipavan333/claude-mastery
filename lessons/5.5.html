5.5 · Scheduled Tasks: Work That Runs Without You

Scheduled tasks: delegation meets the calendar. Work that runs at 6am without you — and the design discipline that keeps unattended automation honest.

In one breath: Cowork tasks can run on schedules — daily briefs, weekly reports, monthly maintenance — turning your proven delegations into standing automation; unattended work demands stricter specs: conservative permissions, quarantines, receipts, and a review habit.

What schedules unlock

The morning brief, automated: your 4.7 sweep (inbox, calendar, priorities) running before you wake — arriving as a finished note, not a prompt you remember to send. Recurring reports: the weekly status, the Monday metrics summary, the monthly client update draft — 5.4's specs on a timer. Maintenance runs: 5.3's organise-pattern re-applied weekly — the Downloads folder that simply never re-chaosifies. Watchers: «every morning, check [sources] for [conditions]; only alert me if triggered» — competitor price moves, new filings, keyword mentions. Quiet until it matters. The promotion rule: only proven delegations get scheduled. A task earns its schedule the way it earned Auto mode (5.2) — clean manual runs first, receipts verified. Scheduling an unproven spec is automating a question mark.

Unattended = stricter, by design

Nobody's watching, so the spec compensates: Conservative permissions: scheduled tasks read, compile, draft, and file — they don't send, delete, or commit irreversible acts. Outputs land as DRAFTS for your review (the human hinge from 4.7, now structural). Self-describing receipts, every run: each execution produces its summary — what it did, what it found, what it quarantined, what failed. An unattended task without a run-log is unauditable by construction. Failure honesty: the spec says what to do when inputs are missing or weird — «if the export file is absent, write NOTHING and flag it; never fabricate from stale data» (3.4's honesty layer, cron edition — staleness is the scheduled task's signature hallucination). Drift review, weekly: five minutes skimming run-logs. Automation rots silently — sources change format, folders move, assumptions age. The review habit is the maintenance contract you sign with yourself.

Designing a schedule portfolio

Start with two: one BRIEF (information TO you — morning sweep) and one MAINTENANCE (order FOR you — folder hygiene). Different failure modes, both low-stakes, fast trust-building. Name them like infrastructure: «Daily brief 06:30», «Weekly folder maintenance — Sun 22:00» — a legible portfolio you can review, prune, and (Track 11) hand to clients as a deliverable. Cost-awareness: every scheduled run spends usage (1.5/5.1's meter) — a daily heavy research run is a real line item. Match cadence to genuine need: does the competitor scan earn its daily slot, or is weekly honest? The compounding shape: your best schedules are your best delegations, which were your best specs, which were your best prompts. The whole course's staircase — prompt → template → skill → delegation → schedule — completes here: craft, running on rails, while you sleep.

Recap

Prove, then schedule; read-and-draft permissions only; every run logs itself; missing inputs fail honestly; and the weekly drift review is part of the automation — craft on rails, standards raised.
