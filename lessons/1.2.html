1.2 · Tokens & Context: The Physics of Claude

Tokens are Claude's atoms; the context window is its working memory. Master these two ideas and half of all «weird AI behaviour» becomes predictable physics.

In one breath: Claude reads and writes in tokens (≈ three-quarters of an English word each), and everything — your chat, files, its answers — must fit in one context window; when it's full, quality drops and old material falls away.

Tokens: the atoms of language

Before Claude sees your message, a tokenizer chops it into tokens — common chunks of characters. «cat» is one token; «unbelievable» might be «un» + «believ» + «able»; code and rare words shatter into more pieces. Handy rules of thumb for English: 1 token ≈ 4 characters ≈ ¾ of a word 100 tokens ≈ 75 words A dense page of text ≈ 400–500 tokens; a 50-page report ≈ 25,000 tokens Tokens matter for two blunt reasons. Money: API usage is priced per million tokens, in and out — and on subscription plans, your usage allowance is consumed token by token too. Space: which brings us to the context window.

The context window: one desk, finite surface

The context window is the maximum number of tokens Claude can consider at once — and here is the part most people never realise: it must hold everything . Your current message. The entire conversation so far. Attached files. The system's own instructions. And the answer being written, which grows token by token. One desk; everything on it. As of July 2026, Claude's standard window is 200,000 tokens (roughly 500 pages), and the newest models support up to 1 million tokens — around 2,500 pages, a small bookshelf. Enormous. And still finite, which produces three effects you will now recognise instantly: Falling off the desk. In very long chats, the oldest material gets summarised or dropped. That name from an hour ago didn't get «forgotten» — it slid off the desk. Crowded-desk degradation. Even before the hard limit, models attend less sharply when the window is stuffed — like proofreading page 900 of a messy pile. Practical rule: quality before quantity in what you include. Every token is rent. That 200-page PDF costs money and attention on every subsequent turn of the conversation, because it sits on the desk the whole time.

The five professional habits

Everything advanced users do about context reduces to five habits: Fresh chat per task. A new conversation = a clean desk. Long meandering chats are where quality goes to die. Curate what you attach. Ten relevant pages beat three hundred vaguely related ones — for cost and accuracy. Summarise and restart. When a long chat sags, ask: «Summarise our decisions and open questions so far.» Paste that into a fresh chat and continue with a clean, dense desk. Front-load what matters. Put large reference material early and keep instructions crisp near the end (much more on placement in Lesson 3.2). Watch the meter. On paid tools you'll see usage indicators; in Claude Code the /context and /compact commands manage the desk explicitly (Track 7).

Recap

Tokens ≈ ¾ of a word and everything rides in one finite context window — so curate what you include, restart long chats with summaries, and think in tokens before you think in prompts.
