1.2 · Tokens & Context: The Physics of Claude

Tokens are Claude's atoms; the context window is its working memory. Master these two ideas and half of all «weird AI behaviour» becomes predictable physics.

Start here

Claude does not read whole words. It cuts text into small pieces called tokens , and one English word is about 1.8 tokens on today's models. Everything Claude can see at one moment sits in its context window : your message, the whole chat, any files, and the answer it is writing. The window is big (one million tokens on current models) but not endless, and answers get worse as it gets crowded. When it is full, the app has to summarise or drop older parts, which is why long chats seem to «forget». A new chat starts with an empty window.

In one breath: Claude reads and writes in tokens (on today's models, a bit over half an English word each — plan on ~1.8 tokens per word), and everything — your chat, files, its answers — must fit in one context window; when it's full, quality drops and old material falls away.

Everyday picture: Claude works at a desk. Everything for the job — your message, the whole chat so far, any files, and the answer it is writing — has to fit on that desk at once. Words are cut into small Lego bricks (tokens) before they go on the desk, and the desk only holds so many bricks. Where it breaks: A real desk keeps what you put on it. When Claude's window is crowded, it can still «see» everything but can pay less attention to the middle, so a full desk hurts quality before it overflows.

Tokens: the atoms of language

Before Claude sees your message, a tokenizer chops it into tokens — common chunks of characters. «cat» is one token; «unbelievable» might be «un» + «believ» + «able»; code and rare words shatter into more pieces. Handy rules of thumb for English on the current models (Anthropic's docs: 1M tokens ≈ 555,000 words ≈ 2.5M characters on the tokenizer introduced with Opus 4.7): 1 token ≈ 2.5 characters ≈ 0.55 words — so tokens ≈ words × 1.8 100 words ≈ 180 tokens A page of ~300 words ≈ 550 tokens; a 50-page report ≈ 27,000 tokens Older models (Opus 4.6, Sonnet 4.6, Haiku 4.5 and earlier) used a leaner tokenizer: ~¾ of a word per token. If you read «1 token ≈ ¾ word» online, that's the old rule. Tokens matter for two blunt reasons. Money: API usage is priced per million tokens, in and out — and on subscription plans, your usage allowance is consumed token by token too. Space: which brings us to the context window.

The context window: one desk, finite surface

The context window is the maximum number of tokens Claude can consider at once — and here is the part most people never realise: it must hold everything . Your current message. The entire conversation so far. Attached files. The system's own instructions. And the answer being written, which grows token by token. One desk; everything on it. As of October 2026, every current Claude model carries a 1,000,000-token window — about 555,000 words, roughly 1,800 pages of ~300 words, a small bookshelf (the previous generation's 200,000-token standard — about 150,000 words on those older models' leaner tokenizer, per the same models-overview page — survives only on older models such as Haiku 4.5). Enormous. And still finite, which produces three effects you will now recognise instantly: Falling off the desk. In very long chats, the oldest material gets summarised or dropped. That name from an hour ago didn't get «forgotten» — it slid off the desk. Crowded-desk degradation. Even before the hard limit, models attend less sharply when the window is stuffed — like proofreading page 900 of a messy pile. Practical rule: quality before quantity in what you include. Every token is rent. That 200-page PDF costs money and attention on every subsequent turn of the conversation, because it sits on the desk the whole time.

The five professional habits

Everything advanced users do about context reduces to five habits: Fresh chat per task. A new conversation = a clean desk. Long meandering chats are where quality goes to die. Curate what you attach. Ten relevant pages beat three hundred vaguely related ones — for cost and accuracy. Summarise and restart. When a long chat sags, ask: «Summarise our decisions and open questions so far.» Paste that into a fresh chat and continue with a clean, dense desk. Front-load what matters. Put large reference material early and keep instructions crisp near the end (much more on placement in Lesson 3.2). Watch the meter. On paid tools you'll see usage indicators; in Claude Code the /context and /compact commands manage the desk explicitly (Track 7).

Recap

Tokens ≈ half a word on today's models (words × 1.8) and everything rides in one finite context window — so curate what you include, restart long chats with summaries, and think in tokens before you think in prompts.

In one line: Claude reads in small pieces called tokens, and only so many fit on its desk at once.
