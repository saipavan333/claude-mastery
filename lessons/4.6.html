4.6 · Research Mode: Cited Reports on Demand

Research mode: Claude as a tireless analyst — dozens of sources, minutes of autonomous digging, and a cited report at the end. Plus the operator's checklist for trusting it.

In one breath: Research mode sends Claude off to search, read and cross-reference many sources autonomously, returning a structured, citation-rich report — you steer it with a good brief and audit it with the 3.4 toolkit.

What it does differently

Autonomous multi-step search: not one query — many, each informed by what the last found. It follows leads, notices gaps, digs where sources disagree. Source breadth: dozens of pages read and cross-referenced; recency handled by live search (your knowledge-cutoff worries from 1.1 mostly dissolve here). Structured, cited output: a report with sections and per-claim citations you can click — 3.4's fact-table discipline, productised. Connected sources too: with connectors attached (next lesson), research can span your Drive and email alongside the public web — «what did WE decide plus what does the market say» in one report. Cost reality: a research run consumes serious usage (many searches, huge reading) — it's a deliberate tool for questions that deserve it, not a reflex for every query. Availability: Pro and up.

Briefing your analyst (the skill transfers whole)

A research run is only as good as its brief — and the brief is just your 2.1 skeleton pointed at investigation: The question, sharpened: «research the EV market» → «What is the realistic 2026 market size, growth rate, and top-3 competitive dynamics for two-wheeler EV batteries in India, for someone deciding whether to enter as a component supplier?» Purpose (2.2's rule 5) transforms what comes back. Scope fences: time («2024 onward»), geography, what to EXCLUDE («not four-wheelers»), source preferences («industry reports and filings over news commentary»). Decision framing: tell it what decision the research serves — reports written toward a decision beat reports written toward a topic. Format contract (2.4): «executive summary with the verdict; findings with per-claim citations; a section on where sources DISAGREE; open questions.» That disagreements section is the professional's favourite — real intelligence lives where sources conflict.

Auditing the report (the 3.4 checklist, applied)

Click the load-bearing citations — the 2–3 numbers your decision leans on. Real link ≠ supporting link (3.4's citation-checker lesson). Check date-sensitivity: is that market figure from 2026 or a 2023 page the search surfaced? Citations carry dates; read them. Mind the source-quality gradient: a vendor's blog and a regulator's filing are not peers. Ask for a source-quality note if the topic is contested. For decisions with money attached: run the hostile critic (3.4) on the report in a fresh chat — «what would a skeptic attack; which claims rest on single sources?»

Recap

Brief it like an analyst (decision, scope, evidence bar, disagreements section), then audit like a partner (click the load-bearing citations, mind the dates, hostile-critic anything with money attached) — delegation plus audit, never delegation alone.
