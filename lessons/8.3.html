8.3 · The Ecosystem: Remote MCP & Registries

The thousand-server world: remote MCP, OAuth-grade trust, registries and directories — navigating the ecosystem like someone who's seen platform booms before.

In one breath: Remote MCP servers run as hosted services (auth via OAuth, data transiting their infrastructure) unlocking the whole SaaS world; registries and directories are how you find them — navigated with vendor-trust questions, because that's what remote servers are: vendors.

Remote MCP: the SaaS half of the world

What «remote» means concretely: the server runs on someone's infrastructure; your client connects over HTTP(S). No local install — connect from any device, always-current, maintained by the vendor. This is how most SaaS tools (project trackers, CRMs, payment platforms) ship their MCP presence. Auth is OAuth-shaped: you grant through the service's own consent screen (like «Sign in with…» flows) — credentials never touch your config; you approve SCOPES (read issues? write comments?) and can revoke from the service side. Cleaner than local key-handling — and the scope screen is your 8.2 checklist's step 3, rendered as UI. READ IT. The trade you're making: convenience and maintenance for DATA TRANSIT — your queries and results flow through the vendor's servers. Which makes remote-server evaluation exactly VENDOR evaluation: who are they, what's their security posture, what do they log, what happens to data (the questions your company already asks about any SaaS — now asked about AI plumbing).

Finding things: registries and directories

The official ecosystem catalogue (the modelcontextprotocol servers repository and its registry efforts) — reference servers and community listings; provenance strongest for official/vendor-maintained entries. Product-level directories: the app's connector directory (4.7) — curated, consumer-grade trust; Claude Code's ecosystem of plugins bundling servers (7.5) — check each bundle's contents. Vendor-direct: increasingly, the tool you already use announces its own MCP endpoint (search «[tool] MCP server» — official docs beat everything). Best provenance of all: the vendor maintaining their own wrap. Community aggregators: awesome-lists and third-party directories — widest catalogue, thinnest vetting. Fine for DISCOVERY; provenance still gets checked at the source repo (8.2's checklist doesn't care where you FOUND it).

Ecosystem navigation heuristics

Prefer, in order: vendor-official → platform-curated → community-maintained-with-history → everything else (which mostly means «build it yourself» — 8.4, often genuinely easier than auditing a stranger's code). Freshness matters double here: an abandoned server wrapping a living API breaks silently or — worse — gets adopted by a new «maintainer». Check last-update and issue activity like you'd check a vendor's pulse. Scope-shop before you connect: two servers for the same tool often differ mainly in the SCOPES they demand — take the one whose asks match your needs (least-privilege as a selection criterion, not just a config). The build-vs-adopt line: adopt for standard tools with official servers; build for YOUR OWN systems and niche needs (next lesson's whole point — and where the money is: everyone can adopt; few can build).

Recap

Remote servers are vendors — read the scope screen like a contract, rank by provenance, check the pulse, unbundle your needs, and keep the vendor answers on the registry card; adopt the standard, build the gaps.
