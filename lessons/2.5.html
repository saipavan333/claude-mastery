2.5 · Let Claude Think: Reasoning & Extended Thinking

Claude can answer instantly — or genuinely think first. Knowing when to buy thinking, and how to ask for it, is a superpower disguised as a checkbox.

In one breath: For multi-step problems, make reasoning explicit — ask Claude to work step-by-step before answering, or switch on extended thinking — and skip it for simple tasks where thinking is just latency and tokens.

Why «think first» changes the answer

Remember the physics: Claude generates token by token, each conditioned on everything before it (1.1, 1.2). When it reasons OUT LOUD before answering, the intermediate steps become part of the context the final answer is conditioned on. Writing «customer ordered on 3 June… policy window is 30 days… today is 9 July… that's 36 days» makes the final «outside the window» almost inevitable — each small correct step narrows what can come next. Blurting the verdict first skips the scaffolding, and errors sneak in. This is chain-of-thought : not a magic phrase but arithmetic about where tokens get spent. Simplest form: «Think through this step by step before answering.» Structured form (with 2.4's tools): «Work through the problem in <thinking> tags, then give the verdict in <answer> tags» — deliberation you can audit, conclusion you can extract. Best form for high stakes: name the steps yourself — «First list the facts. Then the applicable rules. Then apply each rule. Then verdict.» You're lending Claude your checklist.

Extended thinking: the built-in deliberation engine

Modern Claude models have this as a first-class feature. Extended thinking lets the model reason privately — sometimes at length — before responding. In the app it appears as a thinking toggle/mode; you'll see a collapsed «thought process» you can expand. On the API it's a parameter with a token budget (Lesson 9.2). And the 2026 flagship models run adaptive thinking : they decide how much deliberation each query deserves, scaling effort with difficulty. What thinking buys, concretely: fewer arithmetic slips, better multi-constraint juggling (schedules, budgets, rules), more reliable code, deeper analysis of long documents. What it costs: time and tokens — thinking tokens are real tokens (billed on the API; consuming allowance on plans).

The operator's dial

Skip thinking for: lookups, rewrites, formatting, simple drafts, brainstorms. Deliberation adds latency, not quality — a 40-second pause before a synonym is a bug, not rigour. Buy thinking for: anything with arithmetic, many interacting constraints, legal/policy application, debugging, «what's wrong with this plan?» analysis, decisions with real stakes. Escalation path when an answer wobbles (extending 1.3's rule): first ask the same model to think step-by-step; if still shaky, raise thinking effort; only then reach for the bigger model. Thinking is often the cheaper fix. Audit the thinking when it matters. The visible reasoning is a window: skim it for a wrong assumption at step 2 and you can correct THAT precisely — «step 2 is wrong, the fee waives after 90 days, redo from there» — which beats regenerating blind.

Recap

Deliberation is a dial: buy step-by-step or extended thinking for math, constraints and stakes; skip it for speed tasks; audit visible reasoning and correct the step, not the verdict.
