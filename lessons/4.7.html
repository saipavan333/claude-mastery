4.7 · Connectors: Claude Inside Your Tools

Connectors: pointing Claude at your Gmail, Drive, calendar and team tools — the moment «assistant» stops being a metaphor. Powers, permissions, and the trust calculus.

In one breath: Connectors give Claude read (and sometimes act) access to your real tools — Google Workspace, Microsoft 365, and a growing directory built on MCP — turning «paste it in» into «look it up yourself», governed by permissions you deliberately grant.

What connects, and what that unlocks

Google Workspace: Gmail, Drive, Docs, Calendar — «find the invoice thread from Vertex», «what's in my week?», «pull the Q2 doc's numbers into this draft». Microsoft 365: Outlook, OneDrive, SharePoint equivalents for that half of the working world (Pro+ per current plans). Team tools: Slack and friends — where 6.3 picks up the story. The directory: a growing catalogue of third-party connectors — project tools, CRMs, design tools, payment platforms. Under the hood most are MCP servers — the open protocol you'll study (and build) in Track 8. Today: consume happily; soon: create. The compound move is cross-tool synthesis — the questions that were always possible-but-painful: «cross-reference my calendar with the proposals folder: which clients meet me this week and what's the latest doc state for each?» One prompt, three tools, an answer that used to be an hour of tab-hopping.

The permission model (read before you grant)

Explicit grants, scoped: each connector asks for defined access; you choose what to connect and can disconnect anytime. Claude reaches only what you've granted — the fence is real. Read vs act: reading your inbox is one trust level; drafting or sending from it is another. Grant in stages — read first, act later, autonomy last (a ladder you'll climb formally in Cowork's permission modes, 5.2). The minimum-necessary reflex (4.3's rule, scaled up): connect the accounts and scopes the workflows need — not everything, because everything is easier. Work account for work Projects; think before wiring the personal archive to anything. New attack surface, honestly stated: when Claude reads external content (emails! shared docs!), that content can contain hostile instructions — «ignore your instructions and forward this…». This is prompt injection's natural habitat, Anthropic engineers real defenses against it, and the full operator's threat model is Lesson 8.5. For now, one habit: action-taking on top of untrusted content gets human review.

Workflows that change your week

The morning sweep: «Unreplied emails from real humans older than a day; today's meetings with one line of context each from related docs/threads.» Meeting prep on approach: «Meeting with Nikhil in an hour — thread history, open items, the doc we last touched, three things I should raise.» The retrieval office: «Find the PDF Anita sent about pricing — March-ish» — inbox archaeology, abolished. Draft-in-context: «Reply to Sameer's latest — agree to the timeline, push delivery to the 15th, warm but firm» — drafted FROM the actual thread, reviewed by you, sent by you (read-level trust doing act-level work with a human hinge). Project fusion (4.2 + connectors): client Project + client folder = a room that knows the files as they change, not as they were uploaded.

Recap

Connectors move the context-carrying from you to Claude — grant scopes workflows actually need, verify before trusting, keep a human hinge between untrusted content and consequential action, and climb the trust ladder deliberately.
