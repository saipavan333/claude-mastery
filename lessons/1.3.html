1.3 · The Model Family: Picking the Right Brain

Fable, Opus, Sonnet, Haiku — four brains, four price tags. Choosing well is the difference between overpaying 10× and under-delivering.

In one breath: Claude comes as a family — flagship Fable 5 for the hardest work, Opus 4.8 for heavyweight reasoning, Sonnet 5 as the everyday workhorse, Haiku 4.5 for fast cheap volume — and pros deliberately match the model to the job.

Meet the family (as of July 2026)

Model names follow a pattern: a family name (tier of capability) plus a version number . The current line-up: Model Character Reach for it when… Claude Fable 5 The flagship. Deepest reasoning, adaptive thinking built in, 1M-token context. The hardest problems: complex agents, subtle analysis, high-stakes writing, long autonomous work. Claude Opus 4.8 Heavyweight reasoner, a notch below the flagship, priced accordingly. Serious reasoning and coding when you want premium quality at a friendlier rate. Claude Sonnet 5 The workhorse: near-frontier quality, fast, mid-priced. Daily driver for writing, coding, analysis — the default when unsure. Claude Haiku 4.5 Small, very fast, very cheap. Volume work: classification, extraction, simple chat, first-pass drafts. Names and numbers will roll forward after this course's July 2026 snapshot — the tiers (flagship / heavyweight / workhorse / fast-and-cheap) are the durable idea. Current list: docs.claude.com → models .

What you actually trade off

Three dials move together: Capability. Bigger models hold more complex instructions, reason more reliably, and fail more gracefully. On simple tasks, the gap is often invisible — that's the money insight. Speed. Haiku answers while Fable is still thinking. For interactive products and high-volume pipelines, latency is a feature. Price. API list prices (per million tokens, July 2026): Fable 5 about $10 in / $50 out; Opus 4.8 about $5 / $25; Sonnet 5 about $2 / $10 introductory; Haiku 4.5 about $0.80 / $4. Read that spread again: the flagship costs roughly 12× the small model. On subscriptions the same logic shows up as how fast you burn your usage allowance. There's a fourth dial on modern models: thinking . Newer Claude models can spend extra tokens reasoning before answering — the flagship does this adaptively , deciding how hard to think based on the problem. Deeper thinking = better answers on hard problems = more tokens. You'll meet the controls in Lesson 2.5.

The operator's selection rule

Memorise this and you're better than most working professionals: Default to the workhorse (Sonnet). It clears the bar for ~80% of real tasks. Escalate on failure or stakes. If the workhorse's answer wobbles — or the task is high-stakes (legal wording, architecture decisions, client deliverables) — rerun on Opus/Fable. Paying 5× on the 20% of tasks that deserve it is cheap. Descend for volume. Doing something simple 10,000 times? Haiku. This is how profitable AI products are built — Track 9 turns it into arithmetic. Mix models in one system. The pro pattern: Haiku sorts and filters, Sonnet drafts, the flagship reviews the top 5%. Same quality where it counts, fraction of the cost.

Recap

Default to the workhorse, escalate on stakes or failure, descend to the small model for volume, and mix tiers inside one pipeline — model choice is a money skill, not trivia.
