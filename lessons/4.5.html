4.5 · Artifacts: From Sentence to Software

Artifacts: the moment Claude stops describing work and starts handing it to you — documents, dashboards, apps and games, live in a side panel, shareable with a link.

In one breath: Artifacts are real, usable outputs — documents, code, diagrams, interactive web apps — rendered beside the chat, iterated conversationally, and publishable; AI-powered artifacts can even call Claude from inside themselves.

What artifacts are (and when they appear)

An artifact is substantial, standalone output rendered in its own panel beside the conversation — editable, versioned, downloadable. The species: Documents: reports, guides, policies — living drafts you iterate on conversationally instead of regenerating in-chat. Code: scripts and files with syntax highlighting, ready to copy into your world. Diagrams: flowcharts, org charts, timelines — visual thinking on demand. Interactive web apps & games: the headliner — working HTML/JavaScript/React: calculators, trackers, quizzes, dashboards, tools. Claude writes the code; the panel runs it; you use it immediately. They appear automatically for substantial outputs, or on request: «make this an artifact». The iteration loop is conversational — «make the buttons bigger», «add a reset», «dark theme» — and versions let you step back when an edit goes sideways (2.7's iteration, now with an undo).

AI-powered artifacts: apps with a brain

The 2026-era leap: artifacts can call Claude from inside themselves . That means the thing you build isn't limited to fixed logic — it can think: A flashcard app that GENERATES new questions on your weak topics. A customer-reply trainer that role-plays the angry customer and critiques your answer. An intake form that reads the user's free-text answer and routes them. When someone uses your shared AI-artifact, their usage bills to THEIR Claude account, not yours — which makes sharing safe: you built the tool; each user brings their own brain. Let that sink in: you can now build and distribute small AI apps with zero code, zero servers, zero API keys. (It's also the gentlest possible on-ramp to Track 9, where you'll build the grown-up version with the actual API.)

Publishing, and what artifacts are NOT

Share/publish: an artifact can be published to a link anyone can open — instant distribution for your calculator, guide, or tool. Remixing lets others build on it. Honest limits: artifacts are single-page, sandboxed things — no databases (state resets), no server backends, limited external calls. A calculator, quiz, dashboard, or game: perfect. A multi-user app with accounts and persistence: that's real software — Claude Code (Track 7) and the API (Track 9) territory. The strategic frame: artifacts are for PROTOTYPES, personal tools, teaching aids, and client demos. «Ship the idea in an hour, validate, THEN decide if it deserves real engineering» — that sequencing is a money skill (12.1 will lean on it hard).

Recap

Ask for the thing, not text about it: artifacts render working documents, apps and games you iterate conversationally, power with AI, and publish by link — prototype at sentence-speed, graduate to real engineering only when persistence and users demand it.
