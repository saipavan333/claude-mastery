3.5 · Prompt Templates: Build Once, Run Forever

Stop writing prompts. Start owning them. Templates with variables turn your ten best prompts into a personal toolchest that compounds for years.

In one breath: A prompt template is a proven prompt with variable slots — write once, fill the slots forever — and a maintained library of them is the cheapest professional asset you will ever build.

Anatomy of a template

A template = frozen craft + open slots. The frozen part carries everything Tracks 2–3 taught: role, structure, format contract, truth rules, edge-case law. The slots carry what changes per use: template — meeting minutes (example) You are a precise minute-taker for {TEAM_NAME}. From the transcript in <transcript>, produce: 1. DECISIONS — each as: decision | owner | date agreed 2. ACTIONS — each as: task | owner | deadline ({DEFAULT_DEADLINE} if unstated) 3. OPEN — unresolved items with the blocking question Rules: names exactly as spoken; anything ambiguous → OPEN, never guess an owner; no summary prose. <transcript>{TRANSCRIPT}</transcript> copy Conventions that keep templates sane: {SHOUTING_SNAKE} slots — visually unmissable, searchable, and self-documenting when named well ({AUDIENCE}, not {X}). Slot notes: one line per slot at the top of your saved copy — what goes in, with a good example. Future-you is a stranger (2.2's colleague test applies to your own artifacts). Version and date it. v3 beats «final_final2». When a template fails on a new case, the fix goes INTO the template (2.7's post-mortem, aimed at assets), and the version bumps.

The library: ten winners beat a hundred hoards

Where templates live, in rising leverage: Any notes app — fine on day one. Searchable beats organised. Claude Projects (4.2): a «Prompt Library» project whose knowledge contains your templates — ask «run my minutes template on this» and it fills the slots itself. Skills (4.8): the endgame — a template plus its instructions packaged so Claude applies it automatically when relevant. Your library stops being a place you visit and becomes behaviour Claude has. Curation law: a library you don't prune becomes a junk drawer. Keep the ~10 you actually reuse; archive the rest. The test for admission: used three times, worked three times.

Meta-templates: prompts that write prompts

The compounding trick — a template whose OUTPUT is a template: meta-template — the templatiser Here is a prompt that worked well for me, and the output it produced: <prompt>{THE_WINNING_PROMPT}</prompt> <output>{WHY_IT_WAS_GOOD — paste or describe}</output> Convert it into a reusable template: (1) identify what should become variable slots, named clearly; (2) tighten the frozen parts using: explicit format contract, edge-case rules, and truth rules where facts are involved; (3) output the template plus a one-line usage note per slot; (4) list 2 situations where this template would FAIL, so I know its edges. copy That last clause — «where would this fail» — is the professional touch: every tool has edges, and knowing them is what separates a library from a superstition collection.

Recap

Freeze the craft, slot the variables, admit only 3×3 winners, fix failures upstream with version bumps — sessions evaporate, templates compound.
