2.7 · Iteration: The Conversation Is the Prompt

Nobody nails the prompt first try — operators just recover faster. Iteration verbs, surgical feedback, and the restart instinct.

In one breath: Treat the first answer as an opening bid: steer with precise feedback (keep X, change Y, because Z), correct steps rather than regenerating blind, and when a chat's context turns to sludge, summarise and restart fresh.

Feedback that steers (and feedback that doesn't)

«That's not quite right, try again» re-rolls the dice with the same weights. Useful feedback has three parts — keep / change / because : «Keep the structure and the second paragraph exactly. Change the opening — it buries the news; lead with the price change. Because readers decide in the first line whether to keep reading.» The because is not politeness — it's information. It lets Claude generalise your correction to the ten micro-decisions you didn't mention. A vocabulary of surgical verbs beats «improve this»: Verb Use it for Tighten same content, fewer words («tighten by 30%») Punch up / soften energy and tone dials Restructure right content, wrong order («lead with the verdict») Ground add evidence («support each claim with a number from the doc») Vary «give 3 alternatives for the headline only» Continue vs redo «paragraphs 1–2 are perfect — redo only the ending»

Refine, or restart? The sludge test

Iteration has a failure mode: the long, winding chat where corrections pile up, old drafts haunt the context (1.2), and each answer drags residue from six abandoned directions. Signs the desk has turned to sludge: Claude resurrects things you rejected, mixes versions, forgets recent constraints, or the tenth «no, like I said…» produces the same mistake. The restart move (you met it in 1.2; now it's a reflex): ask «Summarise: the goal, the decisions we've locked, the constraints, and what's still open — as a brief I can paste into a fresh chat.» Then restart with that brief + the current best draft. Two minutes; you keep 100% of the decisions and 0% of the sludge. Rule of thumb: three failed corrections on the same point = restart , with that point promoted into the prompt itself (a lesson learned belongs in instructions, not in argument).

Meta-prompting: Claude as your prompt coach

The closing loop of the whole track: Claude is excellent at improving prompts for Claude. Three standing moves: Pre-flight: «Here's my draft prompt: […]. Before answering it, tell me: what's ambiguous, what context are you missing, what would make the output better?» Thirty seconds; catches the gap before the bad output. Post-mortem: after a disappointing answer — «What in my prompt led you there? Rewrite my prompt so it would have produced what I actually wanted.» This turns every failure into a better prompt (and populates your 3.5 library). The ask-me-first pattern: for big tasks, end with «Before starting, ask me up to 3 questions that would most improve the result.» You've delegated the colleague-test (2.2) to the colleague.

Recap

First answers are opening bids: steer with keep/change/because and surgical verbs, restart when context turns to sludge (three strikes), and end every session with the post-mortem that upgrades your prompt library.
