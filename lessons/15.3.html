15.3 · Build-Along: The Content Repurposing Agent

Build-Along #2 — the Content Repurposing Agent: turn one long-form asset into a week of platform-native content in the client's authentic voice, faithful to the source. A productized retainer for every creator and founder who makes more content than they can distribute.

In one breath: The Content Repurposing Agent takes one long-form asset (a post, a transcript, a talk) and produces a week of platform-native pieces — a LinkedIn post, an X thread, a newsletter section, SEO snippets — all matched to a saved voice guide and strictly faithful to the source, delivered to a review folder (never auto-posted), which is why it sells as a productized $500–$2,000/mo retainer to creators, founders, and agencies who make content faster than they can distribute it.

Step 1-2 — Spec, and make the voice guide the core asset

The five parts: Job — on each new long-form asset, produce a week of platform-native pieces in the client's voice, faithful to the source. Trigger — a new asset dropped in a folder, a URL submitted, or a scheduled pull from the content calendar. Loop + tools — read the source, extract the key ideas and quotable lines, reshape each into per-platform drafts guided by a voice guide, assemble into one review doc. Guardrails — faithful to source (no invented stats), on-voice, never auto-publish. Delivery + report — a per-asset folder of drafts by platform, plus a log of what was produced. The voice guide is the product. Before any automation, build a reusable voice guide with the client: their tone (how they sound), five to ten do's and don'ts, banned words and clich s, sentence rhythm, how they open and close, and — most powerfully — three to five example posts they love, because showing beats telling (2.3). Save it as a Skill (4.8) or project memory so every run loads it automatically. This single artifact is why your output sounds like them and a competitor's sounds like a robot — it is your moat and the thing the retainer really pays for.

Step 3 — Build the repurposing loop

Because this is a file-and-document job for one client at a time, Cowork is an excellent no-code fit (give it the source folder, the voice-guide Skill, and a template for the output doc); reach for the Agent SDK only when you productize it for many clients. Build the loop as distinct, verifiable stages: Ingest & understand. Read the source (transcribe first if it's audio/video). Have the agent produce a short 'key ideas' map — the 5–8 core points, the best quotable lines, and the single big idea. Check this by eye; if the map is wrong, every downstream post is wrong. Reshape per platform. For each channel, a dedicated instruction that respects that platform's norms — a LinkedIn post (hook + story + takeaway), an X thread (numbered, punchy), a newsletter blurb, an SEO title + meta description. Each draft is generated through the voice guide, not just about the topic. Assemble. Drop everything into one clean review doc, grouped by platform, each piece labelled and ready to copy — plus a one-line note on which source passage it came from (so the reviewer can fact-check fast). Use a strong model (Sonnet or Opus 5) here — voice quality is the whole value, so this is not the place to cheap out on the model.

Step 4 — Guard against the two failure modes: fabrication and drift

A content agent has two ways to embarrass a client, and your guardrails target both: Fabrication. The fastest way to destroy trust is a confident invented statistic or a claim the source never made. Enforce a hard rule: every factual claim must trace to the source; if a post needs a number or example the source lacks, the agent leaves a bracketed placeholder for the human, never a guess (1.6/10.5). Add a verification pass — 'list every factual claim in these drafts and the source line it came from' — and flag any that can't be traced. Voice drift. Over a long run, outputs slide toward generic-LinkedIn-voice. Counter it by always loading the voice guide with its examples, and by adding a self-check step: 'rate each draft against the voice guide's do's and don'ts and rewrite any that violate them.' Never auto-publish. The agent produces drafts; the human reviews, tweaks, and posts. This keeps final judgment (and legal/brand risk) with a person — and, honestly, is also what keeps the client feeling like an author rather than a bystander.

Step 5 — Package it as a done-for-you content engine

The offer: 'Send me your long-form; get back a week of on-brand, ready-to-post content every time — in your voice, fact-checked to your source.' The demo: run it on one of the client's own past pieces and show the week of drafts — when they read a LinkedIn post that sounds exactly like them, you've sold it. The report: a monthly log — assets processed, pieces produced, hours saved — makes the retainer obvious. Pricing: a productized retainer, roughly $500–$2,000/mo by volume and number of channels, or per-asset; price on time saved and consistency, not tokens (11.4). The honest part: be clear you're selling consistent, on-brand distribution , not guaranteed virality — reach depends on the ideas and the market, which you don't control (12.5). Selling the realistic outcome keeps clients for years; overselling reach loses them in month two. Verify current rate ranges on the Current facts page and Track 11.

Recap

The Content Repurposing Agent turns one long-form asset into a week of platform-native content, and its whole value rests on two things: a reusable voice guide (built from real example posts) that makes output sound authentically like the client, and strict source-faithfulness enforced by a verification pass that traces every claim and uses placeholders instead of guesses. Build it in Cowork (file-based) for one client or the Agent SDK to productize; draft in a strong model because voice quality is the product; and never auto-publish. Sell it as a done-for-you engine priced on consistency and time saved (~$500–$2,000/mo), honestly framing the outcome as reliable on-brand distribution, not guaranteed virality.
