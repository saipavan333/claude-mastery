3.2 · Long-Context Mastery: 200K Tokens Without Tears

A 200K window is a superpower with a user manual. Placement, quote-first grounding, and needle-proofing — the craft of giant contexts.

In one breath: For long-document work: put the big material at the top, instructions at the bottom, demand verbatim quotes before conclusions, and structure multi-document prompts with labelled tags — recall degrades gracefully, so engineer for it.

The physics of recall in long contexts

Within the window, everything is visible — but not everything is equally salient . Behaviours worth engineering around: Edges beat middles. Material at the start and end of the context tends to get sharper attention than the deep middle («lost in the middle» in the research literature). Modern Claude models are strong at needle-retrieval, but salience still isn't flat — on hard synthesis tasks, position matters. Instructions compete with bulk. A one-line instruction floating above 300 pages is a whisper in a stadium. Instructions want to sit AFTER the material, close to where generation begins — Anthropic's own guidance: documents at the top, query at the bottom (this ordering also unlocks caching wins later — 9.6). Structure is a map. Tagged, labelled documents (<doc id='contract_2024'>…) let Claude address material the way an index lets a human — and let YOU say «compare doc contract_2024 with doc contract_2026».

The long-context prompt pattern

Memorise one canonical shape: Top: all documents, each fenced and labelled: <documents><doc id='…' date='…'>text</doc>…</documents> Then: the role and standing rules (2.6). Bottom: the task, the format spec, and the grounding demand: «Before answering, extract the passages relevant to the question into <quotes>, citing doc id. Base the answer ONLY on those quotes; if the documents don't contain the answer, say so.» That quotes-first demand is the single highest-value long-context habit. It forces retrieval before reasoning (a mini-chain inside one prompt — 3.1 thinking), makes every claim auditable in seconds (1.6), and measurably improves accuracy on long-document tasks because the model conditions its answer on re-surfaced text instead of a fading impression.

When NOT to stuff the window

Fit is not the bar; relevance is (1.2). The decision tree: One question, findable in known sections? Send only those sections. Cheapest, sharpest. Synthesis across everything? (themes across 40 interviews, contradictions across contracts) — this is what giant windows are FOR; stuff away, with structure. Repeated questions against the same corpus? Two-stage it: one pass builds a structured index/brief (map-reduce, 3.1); later questions run against the index, dipping into full text only when the index points somewhere (the contract pattern from 1.2's worked example, now formalised). Corpus bigger than any window? That's retrieval territory — search first, read selected chunks (you'll meet RAG properly in Track 9's ecosystem; conceptually it's this bullet at industrial scale).

Recap

Documents top, task bottom, everything fenced and labelled; demand quotes before conclusions and ABSENT over interpolation; stuff the window for synthesis, index the corpus for repeated queries.
