3.7 · The Pattern Library: 25 Battle-Tested Prompts

The whole track, compressed into 25 patterns you can raid — each one a named move with the prompt to run and the reason it works.

In one breath: Twenty-five reusable prompt patterns across five families — clarity, structure, reasoning, truth, and iteration — each distilled from Tracks 2–3 into a form you can deploy in thirty seconds.

Family 1 — Clarity (the 2.1–2.2 moves)

# Pattern The move 1 The Full Skeleton Role + Context + Task + Constraints + Format + Example — deploy for anything you'll actually use. (2.1) 2 The Deliverable Swap Replace the topic with the artifact: «about our policy» → «a 150-word customer-facing paragraph explaining…». (2.2) 3 Numbers-for-Adjectives Every vague adjective becomes a bound: short→under 100 words; a few→exactly 5; better→cut 30%, keep all facts. (2.2) 4 The Edge-Case Law Legislate the mess before it happens: «mixed→classify by complaint; spam→SPAM; unclear→UNCLEAR, never guess.» (2.2) 5 The Purpose Line One sentence of WHY: «this is for the CEO who has 60 seconds» — buys a hundred correct micro-decisions. (2.2)

Family 2 — Structure (the 2.3–2.4 moves)

# Pattern The move 6 Few-Shot Coverage Three examples: one typical, one tricky, one edge — content varies, structure identical. (2.3) 7 Schema by Example One filled sample row beats a page of field descriptions: {"name":"…","amount":123.45}. (2.3+2.4) 8 The Fence All pasted material inside labelled tags — <email>, <policy>, <doc id='x'> — instructions refer to tags by name. (2.4) 9 Labelled Output «Reasoning in <thinking>, result in <answer>» — humans read one, scripts cut the other. (2.4) 10 No-Preamble «No preamble, no closing summary — begin directly with [the first element].» The most-wanted instruction nobody writes. (2.4) 11 Fill-the-Blanks Paste a template with ____ slots: «fill only the blanks, change nothing else.» Strongest pure-chat format control. (3.3)

Family 3 — Reasoning (the 2.5 + 3.1–3.2 moves)

# Pattern The move 12 Named Steps Lend your checklist: «First facts. Then applicable rules. Then apply each. Then verdict.» (2.5) 13 Think-Then-Commit Deliberation in <thinking>, then a committed first line: VERDICT: APPROVE/REJECT/NEEDS-INFO. (2.5+3.3) 14 The Chain Split Two unrelated verbs = two prompts. Extract→compare→recommend, checkpoints between. (3.1) 15 Map-Reduce Same focused prompt per item ×N, then one synthesis across the outputs. Volume's best friend. (3.1) 16 Docs-Top-Task-Bottom The canonical long-context layout: fenced documents up top, role, then task+format at the bottom. (3.2) 17 Quotes-First «Extract relevant passages into <quotes> (with ids), then answer ONLY from them.» Retrieval before reasoning. (3.2) 18 The Panel «Answer as a growth marketer, a CFO, and a burned-out customer. Then reconcile.» Casting as analysis. (2.6)

Family 4 — Truth (the 1.6 + 3.4 moves)

# Pattern The move 19 The Honest Out «If you don't know or the material doesn't say — say so plainly. An honest gap is a good answer.» (1.6/3.4) 20 Provenance Tags Every factual sentence ends [source: doc-id / URL / model knowledge] — the last label aims your verification. (3.4) 21 The Fact Table First Stage 1: claim | source | verbatim quote | date. Human checkpoint. Stage 2: write ONLY from the table. (3.4) 22 The Hostile Critic Fresh chat: «verify every claim against sources: supported / unsupported / contradicted, with the quote or the gap.» (3.4)

Family 5 — Iteration & Assets (the 2.7 + 3.5–3.6 moves)

# Pattern The move 23 Keep-Change-Because «Keep the table and tone. Change the intro to lead with the deadline — because readers skim.» Feedback that steers. (2.7) 24 The Post-Mortem «What in my prompt led you there? Rewrite it so a fresh chat would produce what I wanted in one shot.» Every failure becomes an asset. (2.7) 25 Ask-Me-First «Before starting, ask me up to 3 questions that would most improve the result.» The colleague-test, delegated. (2.7) How to actually use this page: don't memorise — RAID. Bookmark it (or print the Track 3 cheat sheet). When a session disappoints, come here, find the family the failure lives in (clarity? structure? reasoning? truth? iteration?), deploy the pattern. After ten raids you'll notice you stopped needing the page — the patterns moved into your hands. That's the track completing its job.

Recap

Twenty-five moves, five families, one physics: raid the book when sessions disappoint, stack by stakes, and let the patterns migrate from the page into your hands.
