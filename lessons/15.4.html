15.4 · Build-Along: The Research & Intel Agent

Build-Along #3 — the Research & Intel Agent: a scheduled, mostly-unattended agent that monitors a defined set of sources every morning, dedupes what's genuinely new, and delivers a cited briefing with a 'what changed / why it matters' summary. A high-value B2B retainer where accuracy and reliability ARE the product.

In one breath: The Research & Intel Agent runs on a schedule (say every weekday at 7am), sweeps a curated set of sources, dedupes new items against a memory of what it already reported, verifies and cites every claim, and delivers a concise briefing on what changed and why it matters — an unattended, accuracy-first agent that commands a $1,000–$5,000/mo B2B retainer precisely because the product is trustworthy, non-repetitive intelligence, not raw summarization.

Step 1-2 — Spec the scheduled job, then choose a stack that remembers

The five parts: Job — every morning, monitor a defined source set, surface what's genuinely new, and deliver a cited briefing on what changed and why it matters. Trigger — a schedule (weekday 7am). Loop + tools — fetch sources (web/search tools), extract candidate items, dedupe against memory, verify + cite, write the briefing, deliver it. Guardrails — cite everything, flag uncertainty, never fabricate, dedupe rigorously. Delivery + report — a dated briefing (doc or email), an archive, and a source-coverage log so the buyer sees what was watched. The stack must persist state between runs — that's the non-obvious requirement. Deduplication only works if the agent remembers what it already reported. Two good options: Cowork scheduled tasks (5.5) writing a running 'already-reported' file it reads each morning — ideal for running this as a hands-on service for a few high-value clients; or the Agent SDK with sessions/stored state, or Managed Agents (10.4) for hosted, long-running, async operation at scale. Use a cheap model (Haiku) to scan and filter many raw items, and a strong one (Sonnet/Opus) to synthesize the final briefing — you're reading a lot and writing a little.

Step 3 — Build the loop: fetch → dedupe → verify → brief

Build each stage to be reliable on its own, because no human will be watching when it runs: Curate the source list. This is a craft artifact you build with the client: the exact competitor blogs, news feeds, release-notes pages, filings, and forums that matter to them. A tight, high-signal list beats scraping the whole web — and it's part of what they're paying for. Fetch & extract candidates. Pull recent items from each source; extract a normalized record per item (title, source, date, link, one-line gist). Filter obvious noise cheaply with Haiku. Dedupe against memory. Compare candidates to the 'already-reported' store — by URL and by semantic similarity (the same story often appears in three places). Only genuinely new developments survive. This is the step that protects the reader's trust, so test it hard. Verify & cite. For each surviving item, confirm the claim actually appears at the linked source (don't report a headline the article doesn't support), attach the citation, and mark confidence. Anything the agent can't verify gets flagged, not asserted (3.4/1.6). Synthesize the briefing. Group by theme, lead with 'what changed and why it matters,' keep it short and skimmable, and append the source-coverage log. Then update the memory store with what was reported.

Step 4 — Guard an agent nobody is watching

Unattended operation raises the bar on guardrails, because failures ship straight to the client: Cite or don't claim. Every statement in the briefing carries a source link. A claim without a citation doesn't go in — this one rule prevents the worst failure (a confident, wrong, unattributed 'development' that sends the client into a bad decision). Flag uncertainty explicitly. When a source is thin or contradictory, say so ('unconfirmed; single source') rather than laundering it into a clean-sounding fact. Executives can handle 'we're not sure yet'; they can't handle being confidently misled. Fail loudly, not silently. If a source is unreachable or a run errors, the briefing should say 'could not reach X today' rather than quietly omitting it — a silent gap looks like 'nothing happened' and erodes trust when the client finds out otherwise. Log every run. Respect source terms. Honor robots and terms of service; prefer official feeds and APIs. A research service that gets a client blocked or into a legal dispute is worse than no service. The theme: an unattended agent must be conservative — it is better to say less, clearly sourced, than more, unreliably. Reliability is the product.

Step 5 — Package the premium retainer

The offer: 'A cited intelligence briefing on [their market/competitors] in your inbox every morning — only what's new, only what matters, every claim sourced.' The demo: run it for a week for free and hand over five real briefings; the quality and the no-repeats discipline sell themselves. The report: the source-coverage log and archive prove diligence and let them audit you. Pricing: $1,000–$5,000/mo — this is high-value B2B, priced on the cost of missing something important, not on run cost (11.4); a single missed competitor move can dwarf a year of the retainer. Curated source lists and per-client tuning make each new client cheaper to serve, which is the path to an intelligence-service business (11.6). Verify current rate ranges on the Current facts page and Track 11.

Recap

The Research & Intel Agent is a scheduled, unattended build where accuracy and non-repetition ARE the product. It wakes on a clock, sweeps a curated source list, dedupes genuinely-new items against persistent memory, verifies each claim against its source and cites it (flagging or dropping anything unverifiable), synthesizes a short 'what changed / why it matters' briefing, and updates its memory. Because no human watches each run, the guardrails are stricter: cite-or-don't-claim, flag uncertainty, fail loudly, respect source terms, stay conservative. Build it on Cowork scheduled tasks or the Agent SDK/Managed Agents with persistent state; price it as a premium B2B retainer ($1,000–$5,000/mo) on the cost of missing something, not on run cost.
