6.2 · Claude for Excel: Spreadsheets With a Brain

Claude for Excel: the analyst add-in — explains formulas, finds errors, builds models — with cell-level citations, inside the workbook where finance actually lives.

In one breath: Claude for Excel works inside your spreadsheet — answering questions with cell citations, tracing formulas, fixing errors, building and editing models with change tracking — for the world where the .xlsx IS the deliverable.

What it does inside the grid

Answers with cell citations: «why is Q3 margin down?» → an explanation citing B14, the driver cells behind it, and the tab where the assumption lives. Claims you can click — 3.4's provenance discipline, shipped as a product feature. Traces and explains formulas: the 90-character nested monster → plain-English what-it-does, what feeds it, what it feeds. Inherited-workbook archaeology, solved. Finds breakage: errors, circular references, inconsistent formulas across a range (the row where someone hard-coded over the formula in 2023 — the classic silent killer). Builds and edits Excel-natively: models from scratch or into your template — sorting, filtering, pivots, conditional formatting, data validation — with change tracking and explanatory comments , so every edit is visible and reviewable (the receipts principle, 5.3, built in). Boundaries as of July 2026: .xlsx/.xlsm on Excel web, Windows, Mac, iPad; no VBA/macros authoring, and treat it — per Anthropic's own guidance — as a colleague whose work you review, not an auto-signer of final deliverables.

The three jobs it transforms

Archaeology (inherited workbooks): «map this workbook — every tab's purpose, the calculation flow between tabs, and the top 5 cells everything depends on.» The fear-workbook becomes a documented system in minutes. Audit (before the meeting): «check this model for: formula inconsistencies within ranges, hard-coded values where formulas should be, circular refs, and cells that disagree with their column's pattern — list with locations.» Your 5.4 sign-off ritual gains an instrument. Build (5.4's specs, in-grid): assumptions block + live formulas + reconciliation checks — same architecture, now executed where the file lives, with change-tracking as the built-in receipt. Cowork builds workbooks FROM sources at folder scale; the add-in refines and interrogates IN the workbook. They compose.

The operator's Excel discipline

Citations get clicked (the 3.4 reflex): the answer says B14 — you look at B14. Ten seconds; the whole trust model. Changes get reviewed via tracking: the add-in's edits arrive visible — review them like a colleague's, especially on models that move money. Injection awareness, spreadsheet edition: a spreadsheet from outside is stranger-content (a vendor's «template» can carry hostile text in cells — same theory as 6.1's pages). Anthropic's guidance: trusted documents; caution with external files. Your least-privilege instincts apply to workbooks too. The final-deliverable rule: AI-assisted, human-signed. Audit-critical calculations get YOUR verification pass — the tool accelerates the analyst; the analyst still signs (5.4's byline law, now in the grid where it was always most needed).

Recap

The analyst in the grid: archaeology, audit, and reviewed builds — click the citations, track the changes, quarantine stranger-workbooks, and sign the final numbers yourself.
