3.4 · Hallucination Defenses: Engineering for Truth

From habits to engineering: the complete anti-hallucination stack — grounding, citation demands, honest-uncertainty design, and verification loops.

In one breath: Truth is engineered in layers: constrain the task to supplied material, demand citations and confidence labels, give explicit permission to say I-don't-know, verify with fresh-context critics — and match the stack's weight to the stakes.

Layer 1 — shrink the surface where invention can happen

Closed-book vs open-book: the single biggest lever. «Answer ONLY from <doc>» converts an unbounded recall task into a bounded reading task — hallucination surface collapses (3.2's grounding, now understood as a truth tool, not just an accuracy tool). Search-then-answer for the open world: anything recent, priced, versioned, or niche gets «search the web first; cite sources» (1.6 defense 2 — now a standing rule in your system prompts, not a per-chat rescue). Structured nulls beat prose gaps: extraction contracts' null-honesty (3.3) IS anti-hallucination — schema design and truth design are the same design. Decompose high-invention tasks (3.1): «write a report with statistics» invites invented statistics; chain it — gather stats (with sources) → verify checkpoint → write from verified list. The checkpoint is a truth valve.

Layer 2 — make uncertainty speakable, then legible

The permission clause, upgraded: «If you don't know or the material doesn't say: state that plainly. An honest gap is a GOOD answer.» Works because trained helpfulness treats non-answers as failures unless you re-price them. Confidence labels as output schema: every claim tagged certain / likely / should-verify (1.6 defense 4) — but now REQUIRED BY FORMAT, so skipping the label breaks the contract (3.3's trick pointed at truth). Source-per-claim: «each factual sentence ends with [source: doc-id / URL / 'model knowledge']». That last option matters: forcing Claude to LABEL claims that come from its own training (vs your documents) makes the risky category visible — model-knowledge claims are where your verification minutes go. Numbers get provenance: standing rule — «every number: exact quote of where it came from, or label it an estimate WITH the estimation basis.» Invented-number incidents drop to near zero under this one rule.

Layer 3 — adversarial verification loops

For work that ships, add a hostile pass — the generator–critic chain (3.1) with teeth: The extraction audit: fresh chat, paste output + sources: «Verify every claim against the sources. Output: claim | supported/unsupported/contradicted | the quote or the gap.» Mechanical, fast, brutal. The assumption hunter: «List every assumption this analysis rests on that is NOT stated in the source material, ranked by how much the conclusion changes if the assumption is wrong.» Finds the invisible load-bearing guesses. The citation checker: for search-based work — «visit each cited source; does it actually say what the answer claims it says?» (Citations can be real links to pages that don't support the claim — the subtlest failure class.) Stack-weight by stakes (1.6's triage, now a dial): green work = layer 1 only; yellow = +labels; red = full stack + human on the flagged items. Over-engineering a brainstorm and under-engineering a client deliverable are the same mistake in opposite directions.

Recap

Engineer truth in layers — shrink the invention surface, make uncertainty speakable and provenance visible, verify adversarially in fresh contexts — and weight the stack by stakes, because both over- and under-engineering are failures.
