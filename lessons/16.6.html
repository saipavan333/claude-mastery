16.6 · Sell a Vertical: Industry Plugins & the Packaged Agent

The market's verdict, and your move: the 2026 wave of industry plugins (Salesforce, financial advisors, small business, teachers) proves buyers want vertical, connector-rich agents — here's how to package, price, and sell one.

In one breath: In 2026 Anthropic shipped a wave of vertical offerings — Salesforce sales skills, a financial-advisor plugin with connectors to the tools advisors already use, a small-business bundle of workflows and integrations, and a free Enterprise offering for teachers — which together prove where applied-AI money concentrates: not in generic chat but in agents pre-wired into a specific industry's tools and workflows, and this lesson shows you how to package, price, and sell a vertical agent of your own.

Read the market's tell: why verticals win

Each 2026 plugin encodes the same three things a generic assistant lacks, and they are exactly what a buyer pays for: Connectors to the tools the work already lives in. The financial-advisor plugin's value isn't a smarter model — it's being wired into the custodial, portfolio, and planning systems advisors use daily (the 4.7/8.1 connector idea, verticalized). Integration is the moat. Prebuilt, domain-shaped skills. Salesforce shipped with dozens of ready sales skills (manage accounts, opportunities, pipeline) — the buyer doesn't want a blank prompt, they want their job's tasks already encoded (16.4's Skills, aimed at a role). Workflow and compliance fit. The teacher and small-business bundles fit real workflows and constraints (privacy, roles, the actual sequence of a day's work). Generic tools ignore this; vertical tools are designed around it. You cannot out-model Anthropic, and you don't need to. The opportunity these plugins reveal is the long tail they'll never reach: the thousands of specific niches, local markets, and idiosyncratic workflows too small for a big vendor and perfect for an operator who knows that domain.

Package a vertical agent

Turn the pattern into your offer using everything this course built: Pick a niche you understand (11.2) — narrow beats broad: not «AI for lawyers» but «lease-abstraction for commercial property managers.» Depth in one workflow is what you can actually deliver and defend. Wire the connectors (Tracks 8–9) — MCP servers and APIs into the specific tools that niche uses; integration is the value, so this is where the work goes. Encode the domain as Skills (16.4) — the niche's recurring tasks as packaged, tested Skills that load on cue. Build the loop and guard it (Tracks 10, 15) — drafts-not-sends on consequential actions, injection defenses, verification, a human gate on the irreversible. Make it legible (15.x) — the digest/report that lets a non-technical buyer see it working is what earns the retainer. That stack — niche + connectors + Skills + guarded loop + legible reporting — is a vertical agent, and it's defensible precisely because it's specific: the moat isn't the model, it's your domain knowledge encoded into integrations and guardrails a generalist can't easily copy.

Price and sell it (honestly)

Pricing follows Track 11: value-based, not token-based. A vertical agent that saves a firm days of skilled labor a month is priced against that value — a setup fee (integrate, encode the niche, tune) plus a monthly retainer to run, monitor, and improve it (typical shapes: four-figure setups, three-to-four-figure monthlies, scaled to the value and volume; verify your market's rates). Selling follows the whole Operator phase: demo on the buyer's own data (16.1's live proof), lead with the connectors and the domain fit (not the model), and be honest about scope (12.6) — you're selling a reliable, integrated, verifiable workflow agent, not magic or guaranteed outcomes. The 2026 plugins are your credibility tailwind: «Anthropic ships vertical agents for Salesforce and financial advisors — I build the same shape for your specific corner of [industry], integrated with your tools.» That sentence, backed by a working demo and a verification story, is a business.

Recap

Anthropic's 2026 vertical wave — Salesforce sales skills, a financial-advisor plugin, a small-business bundle, free access for teachers — reveals where applied-AI money concentrates: connector-rich, domain-skilled, workflow-fit agents, not generic capability. You can't out-model the frontier, so you serve the long tail: pick a niche you understand, wire its tools (Tracks 8–9), encode its tasks as Skills (16.4), build a guarded loop with legible reporting (Tracks 10, 15), and price on value (Track 11). Lead with integration and domain fit, prove it live on the buyer's data, and be honest about scope — that's a defensible vertical-agent business, and the summit this whole course was climbing toward.
