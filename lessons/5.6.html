5.6 · Power Patterns: Projects, Instructions, Sub-agents

Projects, standing instructions, and sub-agents: the pieces that turn a pile of clever delegations into an organised, compounding operation.

In one breath: Cowork scales through structure — Projects give delegations rooms with files and standing instructions, folder rules encode house law, and sub-agents parallelise big jobs — your one-person operation, organised like a firm.

Cowork Projects: rooms for your operation

The 4.2 idea, upgraded with hands: a Cowork Project bundles related tasks with shared files, instructions and memory — but here the room's occupant DOES things. The «Client Zenith» project holds their folders, voice rules, and delivery specs; every session inside starts briefed AND equipped. Project memory makes sessions compound: decisions and context persist across sessions within a project (the 4.3 layer, scoped to the room) — «continue the migration from where Tuesday's session stopped» actually works. One project per ongoing OPERATION — per client, per recurring pipeline (the Monday-report machine), per life-domain. Same admission test as 4.2: five-plus sessions sharing context. Disposable errands stay roomless.

Standing instructions: house law, written once

Global instructions (all Cowork work) carry your constitution: «move never delete; ambiguity → Review/; every task ends with a receipt; drafts never sends.» Your 5.1–5.5 hard-won rules, promoted from per-prompt to ALWAYS. Folder instructions carry local law: «_originals/ is read-only»; «this client's files never leave this folder tree.» Location-scoped rules that fire regardless of which task wanders in — you'll recognise the pattern as CLAUDE.md thinking (7.3) arriving early. The layering discipline (2.6's altitudes, completed): global law → project law → folder law → task spec. Each layer holds what's true at ITS scope — and when you correct the same thing twice at task level, it graduates upward (the 2.7/4.2 promotion habit, now with four floors).

Sub-agents: the big-job multiplier

What they are: for large tasks, Cowork can split work into parallel workstreams — sub-agents each handling a slice (one per client folder; one per document batch), coordinated toward your outcome. Your 3.1 map-reduce, executing itself. Why you care beyond speed: sub-agents keep slices ISOLATED — each works its own context without cross-contamination (the clean-desk principle, 1.2, applied organisationally), then results merge. Big messy jobs stay coherent. Your management surface stays the same: outcome spec, quarantines, receipts — but add a MERGE check: «after parallel processing, verify the combined output's totals reconcile against per-slice receipts.» Parallel work's signature bug is seams; the spec inspects the seams. Where this goes: orchestrated sub-agents are the beating heart of Track 10's architectures (orchestrator–workers, literally). Cowork gives you the managed version; you're learning multi-agent design with guardrails included.

Recap

Rooms for compounding context, law layered at four altitudes, sub-agents with schema-coordination and seam checks — talent is rented, systems are yours, and the operation is the asset.
