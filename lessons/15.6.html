15.6 · Package, Price & Sell Your Agents

Package, Price & Sell Your Agents — the go-to-market for agents specifically: the outcome-led offer, the demo that closes (run it on their data), setup-plus-monitoring pricing, the reliable delivery-and-handoff, how to find your first buyer, and how one working agent becomes an agency.

In one breath: Selling agents is different from selling gigs or apps because you're selling an ongoing, trusted capability, so the winning motion is: lead with the outcome, prove it with a demo on the buyer's own data, price it as setup-plus-monitoring-retainer on the value delivered, deliver it with guardrails and a report that make trust visible, land your first buyer in a business you already know, and then expand agent-by-agent inside each account until a single working build has become an agency.

The offer and the demo: sell the outcome, prove it on their data

Lead with the outcome, never the technology. 'I'll build you an AI agent' invites fear and skepticism; 'you'll never lose a lead to a slow reply again,' or 'a cited intelligence briefing every morning,' or 'your team stops answering the same fifty tickets' — those sell, because they name a result the buyer already wants (11.2). Write your offer as a promised outcome plus the reliability that makes it safe: the result, delivered dependably, with you watching it. The demo closes the deal, and the rule is simple: run it on the buyer's own data. A generic demo proves nothing; the same agent run on their inbox, their past tickets, their content lands differently — it stops being a claim and becomes a preview of their life with the agent. Best of all is a short shadow-mode run: operate the agent alongside their current process for a week and hand over the artifacts — the drafts it would have sent, the briefings it produced, the tickets it would have deflected — with the safety story attached ('and it escalated every refund to a human'). Seeing real output on real data, safely, is what converts interest into a signed retainer.

Pricing: setup plus a monitoring retainer, on value

The durable model is setup + monitoring retainer (15.1), and it's worth understanding why it fits agents so well. The setup fee ($500–$10,000 depending on complexity and buyer) is paid for the build, integration, and tuning — real work with a clear deliverable. The monthly retainer ($200–$5,000+) is paid for something the buyer genuinely needs: an agent in production requires monitoring, tuning as the world changes, KB and source upkeep, and someone accountable when it hiccups. You're not inventing a fee to be recurring — the recurring work is real, and so is the recurring value (the agent keeps saving hours every month). Price on value, not cost (11.4): anchor to hours saved, leads not lost, tickets deflected, decisions not missed — the API bill is a rounding error against those. Offer two or three tiers (by volume, channels, or seats) so buyers self-select. And resist the race to the bottom: your price reflects reliability and trust, which the weekend-clone competitor can't match — compete there, not on being cheapest. Deliver and handoff: make the trust visible and durable

Delivery is where agent businesses are won or lost, because the buyer's real, ongoing question is 'can I trust this?' Answer it continuously: Ship the report, always. Every agent leaves a visible trail — a digest, a dashboard, an archive (15.1). This is what justifies the retainer month after month; without it, the agent's work is invisible and the fee feels optional. Make the guardrails legible. Tell the buyer, in plain language, what the agent will never do without a human (send, refund, publish, delete) and show them the approval queue. The limits are a feature — they're why it's safe. Handoff and documentation. Give them a short runbook: what it does, how to approve/override, how to reach you, what the escalation paths are. Professionalism here is what separates a retainer from a one-off. Be honest about limits and disclosure. Say what the agent can't do, disclose that it's AI-assisted where customers interact with it, and handle their data responsibly (11.7). Under-promising and over-delivering is how retainers survive year two.

First buyer, then agency: land and expand

Your first buyer should be a business you already understand — your employer, a former client, a friend's company, your own side business. You know their painful recurring job, you have the trust to get a shadow-mode run, and a warm first customer beats cold outreach every time (11.3). Pick the agent that scores greenest for them (usually inbox triage, 15.2) and land it. One reference customer with a real deflection stat or a 'we stopped losing leads' testimonial is worth more than any amount of marketing. Then expand, because agents scale two ways. Within an account: once a client trusts one agent, the second (research, support, content) is far easier to sell — you've already answered the trust question and done the integration, so each additional agent is nearly pure margin. Across accounts: templatize the engine and keep only the per-client config bespoke (the business brief, the voice guide, the source list, the KB) so each new client is faster to serve than the last — that's the move from freelancer to agency (11.6). Stack a handful of retainers and you have a real business with software economics and service stickiness. Every build-along in this track was a product line; this lesson is how you sell them, and how one working agent becomes many.

Recap

Selling agents is selling trusted, ongoing capability, so the go-to-market is specific: lead with the outcome (not the tech), prove it with a shadow-mode demo on the buyer's own data plus the safety story, and price it as setup + monitoring retainer anchored to value (hours saved, leads not lost, tickets deflected) — never to token cost. Deliver with a visible report, legible guardrails, a runbook, and honest disclosure so trust is continuous. Land your first buyer in a business you already know (warm beats cold for a trust-heavy sale), then expand agent-by-agent within accounts and templatize across them — which is how one working build-along becomes an agency with software margins and service stickiness.
