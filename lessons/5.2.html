5.2 · First Session: Folders, Permissions, the Task Loop

Folders, the three permission modes, and the task loop — Cowork's control surfaces, and how a professional sets each dial.

In one breath: You grant Cowork specific folders (nothing else), choose a permission mode per task — Manual (approve each action), Auto (autonomous with safety checks), or Skip (no checks) — and steer through a live task loop of plan, progress, and interventions.

Reach: folders, connectors, and the fence

Explicit folder grants. Cowork touches only directories you've connected — the fence from 4.7, applied to your disk. Operator habit: per-project grants , not «my whole home directory» — matching reach to task (minimum-necessary, the recurring law). Session vs local: sessions run in an isolated environment on Anthropic's servers; local folders bridge through the desktop app (keep it open when the task needs your disk or browser). Cloud-side work continues laptop-closed. Folder-specific instructions: you can attach standing instructions to a folder («never touch _originals/») — a CLAUDE.md-shaped idea you'll meet fully in 7.3; here it's a permission expressed as prose. Connectors and the browser ride along: a Cowork task can read your granted Drive, drive Chrome (6.1's powers, delegated). Same grants, same ladder-of-trust thinking (4.7).

The three modes: match autonomy to reversibility

Mode Behaviour Use when… Manual Pauses for your approval before actions New task types; anything touching originals; your calibration week Auto Proceeds autonomously; built-in safety checks block clearly unsafe actions; deletions still require you The daily default once a task type is proven — speed with a net Skip No approvals, no automatic checking Rarely: fully-reversible sandboxes, copies-only work, where you've decided review costs exceed risks The selection principle you've been building since 1.4: autonomy scales with reversibility. Copy-and-organise in a duplicated folder? Auto, happily. Anything that sends, deletes, or edits sole-copies? Manual — or restructure the task so it CAN'T (work on copies; output to new folders; quarantine ambiguity). The best permission decision is often a task-design decision. Note Auto burns more usage than Manual (more autonomous exploration) — one more reason mode choice is a real decision.

The task loop: managing without hovering

The plan is your first checkpoint. Cowork proposes its approach before grinding — read it. Thirty seconds here catches mis-specifications before they compound (cheaper than any recovery). Progress is glanceable, interruptible: you can watch, or check in periodically, or steer mid-task («skip the 2019 files», «that naming is wrong — use YYYY-MM») — the keep/change/because craft (2.7) works on running work. Parallel sessions: multiple Cowork tasks can run simultaneously — the moment you genuinely feel like a manager with a small team. Manage like one: don't launch five unproven task types at once. The review ritual: every session ends with your audit of the receipt artifact you specified (5.1). Green? Raise that task type's autonomy next time. Surprises? Tighten the spec — and log what you learned (the 2.7 post-mortem, delegation edition).

Recap

Three dials, set per task: reach (minimum folders, copies where possible), mode (autonomy scales with reversibility, upgraded on evidence), and the loop (read the plan, steer mid-flight, audit the receipt).
