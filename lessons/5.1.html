5.1 · From Chatbot to Coworker

Cowork is the shift from answering to finishing: describe an outcome, step away, come back to completed work. Understanding the shift is understanding the decade.

In one breath: Cowork is Anthropic's agentic workspace — Claude plans and executes multi-step tasks (files, documents, research, browsing) in sessions that keep running on Anthropic's servers even when you close your laptop, with you steering through permissions.

What actually changed

Three mechanical shifts, each with consequences: Multi-step autonomy. Cowork decomposes your outcome into steps and executes them — reading files, creating documents, revising, verifying — without a prompt per step. Your 3.1 chains, running themselves; your prompts graduate from «do this step» to «here is done». Real reach. Through the desktop app, Cowork reads and writes YOUR folders (the ones you grant). Outputs land as actual files — spreadsheets with formulas, formatted documents, organised directories — not chat text you copy out. It can also drive the browser and use your connectors (4.7). Sessions that outlive your attention. Cowork runs remotely on Anthropic's servers: close the laptop, the work continues; check from your phone; return to finished output. Delegation with an actual clock-off. Where it lives: Pro plans and up; desktop app is the full experience (local folders); web and mobile let you start and steer sessions anywhere.

The management mindset (your job changed)

With a coworker, your leverage moves from doing to specifying and reviewing : Outcome specs beat step lists. «A folder called Clean/ with one merged PDF per client, named Client_YYYY.pdf, plus an index.xlsx of what went where» — the 2.2 deliverable-naming rule, now aimed at file systems. Acceptance criteria are your new prompt skill: how will the coworker know it's done and RIGHT? «Every source file accounted for in the index; nothing deleted, only copied» — 3.3's totality law, escaped from schemas into the real world. Checkpoints still belong to you (3.1): Cowork shows its plan and progress; you steer mid-flight or review at the end. Delegation ≠ abdication — the 1.6 triage decides how closely you watch which tasks. Usage is the meter to respect: agentic sessions burn far more allowance than chat (many steps × many tokens — 1.5's warning made real). Delegate what's WORTH a coworker's afternoon.

What to delegate first (and what not yet)

Great first delegations: folder cleanups and reorganisations; extract-and-compile jobs (many files → one structured output); format conversions at scale; research-and-synthesise with files; first drafts of document sets. High tedium, clear success criteria, low blast radius. Not yet (until 5.2's permissions and 10.6's judgment): anything that deletes, sends, posts, or pays without review; tasks whose success you can't specify; work on files with no backup. The coworker is capable; the MANAGER earns trust gradually — in both directions.

Recap

Cowork finishes instead of answering: specify outcomes with rules, quarantines and receipts; review at checkpoints; start with tedious low-stakes work; respect the usage meter — you're a manager now.
