4.2 · Projects: Your Team of Specialists

Projects: separate workspaces with their own standing instructions and their own library of knowledge. This is where your craft stops being retyped.

In one breath: A Project bundles custom instructions (a standing system prompt) with uploaded knowledge (documents Claude consults in every chat inside it) — build one per client, per domain, per recurring job, and your context stops evaporating.

The two organs of a Project

Custom instructions — the Project's standing system prompt (2.6, now with a permanent home): who Claude is here, the voice, the rules, the formats. Written once, governing every chat inside. Knowledge — files you upload to the Project itself: brand guides, price lists, past work, templates (3.5!), policies. Claude consults them across all the Project's chats — your reference shelf, always in reach. Together they create compounding context : each chat starts where your best-briefed chat would have started. And chats inside stay organised under the Project — findability solved for free (4.1's landfill problem, structurally fixed).

What deserves a Project (and what doesn't)

The admission test: will I have five or more conversations sharing the same context? Per client — instructions carry their voice and boundaries; knowledge carries their briefs, past deliverables, price list. (The Track 11 freelancer's backbone.) Per recurring job — «Weekly report», «Hiring», «Content pipeline»: instructions carry the format contracts (2.4), knowledge carries the templates and examples. Per domain of life — «My finances», «Kid's school», «Learning Claude» (yes, make one for this course — knowledge: your notes; instructions: your level and goals). The Prompt Library Project (3.5's endgame): knowledge = your template file; instructions = «when I name a template, fetch it from knowledge, ask for the slot values, then run it.» Your toolchest, self-serving. Not Project-worthy: one-off questions, disposable tasks — a Project per errand is bureaucracy. And know the escalation path: when a Project's instructions grow procedural («always do X then Y with files like this»), that's a Skill trying to be born (4.8).

Writing instructions that age well

Stable facts only. Instructions are the constitution; today's task never goes in it (2.6's law). If you edit instructions weekly, something belongs in knowledge or in chats instead. Reference the knowledge explicitly: «pricing questions: answer ONLY from pricelist.pdf; if absent, say so» — grounding rules (3.4) pointed at the shelf. Carry your truth rules: the three standing truth-rules you wrote in 3.4's practice belong in every serious Project's instructions. Maintain like a template: when you correct Claude the same way twice inside a Project, the correction graduates into the instructions (2.7's post-mortem, aimed at the room).

Recap

One room per recurring context: constitution (stable instructions + truth rules) plus shelf (curated, dated knowledge) — brief once, compound forever, and graduate repeated corrections into the room itself.
