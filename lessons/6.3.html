6.3 · Claude in Slack & Microsoft 365

Claude in Slack and Microsoft 365: the assistant becomes a colleague-in-common — thread summaries, drafted replies, meeting prep — and the etiquette of AI in shared rooms.

In one breath: Claude integrates into Slack and the Microsoft 365 world — summarising threads, drafting in-context, answering against team knowledge — turning private leverage into shared leverage, with team norms doing the safety work permissions can't.

What team-surface Claude does

Thread catch-up: «summarise this thread — decisions, open questions, who owes what» — the 60-message archaeology, solved in the room where it lives. Drafts in context: replies that have READ the thread — tone-matched, history-aware (4.7's draft-in-context, team edition; the human hinge still applies: you post, not it). Team knowledge answers: connected to the workspace's docs and channels, it answers «what did we decide about X?» from where the deciding actually happened — institutional memory, queryable. Meeting-adjacent work: agendas from threads, minutes from transcripts (your 4.8 minutes-skill firing in shared context), action-item extraction with owners — the 5.4 artifacts, sourced from group conversation. Where it lives: Slack integration (add Claude to the workspace; @-mention it in threads) and Microsoft 365 (Outlook/Teams/OneDrive-world connections per plan — Pro+ for the M365 connector; Team/Enterprise for the full workspace story). Exact capabilities move fast — the release-notes habit (4.1) applies double on team surfaces.

What changes when AI enters shared rooms

Visibility of queries: in a channel, your @Claude question may be visible to the room — clarity is now a public good (and your 2.2 skills are on display; teams learn prompting from each other's visible prompts, which is genuinely how adoption spreads). Shared context ≠ shared permissions: what Claude can read in a workspace follows the workspace's access rules — but NORMS must cover what permissions can't: is it okay to AI-summarise a sensitive HR thread? To draft-reply on someone's behalf? Teams that never discuss this discover disagreements at the worst moment. The minutes-and-memory shift: when any thread is summarisable on demand, «I'll catch up later» becomes real, meetings need less recap, and WRITING THINGS DOWN WHERE CLAUDE CAN SEE THEM becomes the team's highest-leverage habit — ambient documentation, finally incentivised. Etiquette floor (propose these to any team): AI drafts are labelled as drafts until a human owns them; summaries of people-sensitive threads need the thread's consent; the bot answers questions — humans make announcements. Small norms, most of the safety.

Recap

Team Claude turns channels into queryable memory — catch up with citations, draft with a human hinge, label until owned, keep announcements human, and set the five norms before you need them.
