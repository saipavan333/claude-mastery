2.3 · Show, Don't Tell: The Power of Examples

Few-shot prompting: the single highest-leverage technique in the craft. One good example beats three paragraphs of description.

In one breath: Showing Claude 1–5 examples of input→output (few-shot prompting) transfers format, tone and judgment far better than describing them — because the model is a pattern-continuation engine and examples ARE patterns.

Why examples dominate descriptions

Lesson 1.1 again: Claude continues patterns. A description of what you want («friendly but professional tone, concise, with a specific next step») must be interpreted — and every term («friendly»? «concise»?) is a distribution of possibilities. An example is the pattern — tone, length, structure, punctuation, level of formality, all encoded at once, unambiguously. Vocabulary for the field: Zero-shot: instructions only, no examples. Fine for common, well-defined tasks. One-shot: a single example. Massive upgrade for format-sensitive tasks. Few-shot (multishot): 2–5 examples. The professional standard for classification, extraction, style-matching and any output you'll process downstream.

The craft of choosing examples

Examples are so powerful they transmit your mistakes too. The selection rules: Make them representative of real inputs — not just the easy cases. If real customer emails ramble, your example inputs should ramble. Cover the variation: one typical case, one tricky case, one edge case. Three well-chosen examples beat six similar ones. Keep them consistent in exactly the properties you want copied — format, length, labels. If your examples' outputs vary in structure, Claude will vary too (it can't tell your inconsistency from intent). Show the hard-case behaviour you legislated in 2.2: include an example where the right answer is «UNCLEAR» — now the never-guess rule has a demonstration, not just a description. Wrap them in structure: label them clearly (Example 1: / Input: / Output:) — and in Lesson 2.4 you'll upgrade to XML tags that make the boundaries machine-crisp.

When examples mislead

Two failure modes worth respecting: Example overfit: give three examples of complaints about shipping and Claude may start finding shipping complaints everywhere. Vary the content of examples; keep the format identical — content varies, structure repeats is the golden combination. Stale anchoring in creative work: for brainstorming or original writing, examples can gravity-well the output into imitations. There, prefer describing the quality bar and constraints, or give examples of what to avoid. Rule of thumb: examples for consistency tasks , freedom for creativity tasks .

Recap

Show, don't tell: 1–5 examples with varied content and identical structure transfer format, tone and judgment better than any description — but keep examples away from tasks that need originality.
