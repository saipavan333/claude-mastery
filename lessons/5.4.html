5.4 · Office on Autopilot: Docs, Sheets, Decks

Documents with structure, spreadsheets with working formulas, presentations with design — Cowork as the office suite operator, and you as the editor who signs off.

In one breath: Cowork produces real office artifacts — formatted Word docs, Excel sheets with live formulas, PowerPoint decks — from outcome specs; your craft is the brief (audience, structure, source-grounding) and the sign-off ritual.

Documents: brief like a chief of staff

The spec stack you already own, aimed at .docx: audience + purpose (2.2's rule 5 — «for the CFO, to unlock budget» changes everything), structure contract (2.4 — sections, lengths, what leads), voice (2.6/4.8 — your style skill fires here), and grounding («every figure from the attached sources, cited; gaps stated, not filled» — 3.4, forever). Multi-document jobs are Cowork's edge: «from these 12 project files, produce the quarterly review doc» — reading across sources and synthesising into ONE formatted artifact is exactly what delegation does that chat-pasting can't. Iterate on the file, in place: revisions are conversational («section 3 tighter; move risks before budget») and can be made directly to the document — 2.7's verbs, operating on real files.

Spreadsheets: formulas, not frozen numbers

Demand LIVE formulas. The amateur output is a sheet of pasted values; the professional spec says «projections as formulas referencing the assumptions block, so changing an assumption recalculates everything.» A model, not a snapshot. The assumptions block is non-negotiable: every input the client might question — growth %, costs, rates — in one labelled area, referenced everywhere else. This is 3.4's provenance discipline expressed in spreadsheet architecture (and it's what makes the artifact survive its first hostile meeting). Verification, spreadsheet edition: reconciliation rows («total must equal sum of parts»), a sanity tab, and YOUR spot-check of 3 formulas by clicking into them (4.4's law: numbers come from computation — here, verify the computation wiring itself).

Decks: argument first, slides second

Spec the argument, not the decoration: «10 slides for [audience]: 1 the pain quantified, 2–3 the evidence, 4 the proposal, 5 economics, … each slide = one claim + its support; appendix for detail» — a deck is a document with a rhythm (2.4's shape-first thinking at presentation scale). Source-bound content: deck numbers inherit the same grounding rules as everything else — a beautiful slide with an invented figure is a career incident wearing good design. The sign-off ritual (all three artifact types): before anything leaves — read as the AUDIENCE (does slide 4 answer their objection?), spot-check the load-bearing numbers to sources, and run the hostile-critic pass (3.4) on anything high-stakes: «what would the toughest person in that room attack?»

Recap

Brief the argument, ground every figure, demand formulas over snapshots with one assumptions block, keep one source of truth across artifacts — then earn the byline: audience-read, load-bearing check, hostile critic.
