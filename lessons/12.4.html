12.4 · Selling Skills, Plugins & MCP Servers

Selling skills, plugins, and MCP servers: the lighter-weight products you can build and sell from your course skills — packaged expertise that scales without the full SaaS build.

In one breath: Beyond full products, you can sell packaged EXPERTISE — Skills (4.8), plugins, and MCP servers (8.4) that others install and use — a lighter-weight product form where your judgment, encoded once, sells repeatedly through the ecosystem's distribution.

Skills as products (encoded expertise, packaged)

What you're selling (4.8): a Skill is a packaged procedure — your expertise (a proven workflow, a quality checklist, a specialized capability) encoded so Claude applies it. As a product, it's YOUR judgment about how to do something well, serialized and sellable. What sells: skills that encode SCARCE JUDGMENT for a specific need — 'a skill that reviews contracts for a specific industry's risks', 'a skill that formats financial models to a firm's standard', 'a skill that writes in a specialized compliant style'. The value is the encoded EXPERTISE (what to check, what to never do, the edge cases — the invisible 40% amateurs omit), not just the format. Who buys: teams and individuals who need that expertise applied consistently — a skill pack for a profession (accountants, lawyers, marketers) installs your expertise into their AI, saving them from developing it. You sell your judgment; they install it. The trust dimension: since skills can carry executable steps (4.8), buyers need to trust them — which means YOUR verification and security discipline (your moat) makes your skills trustworthy where others' are risky. Well-documented, verified, secure skills from a credible source command premium.

Plugins and MCP servers (integrations as products)

Plugins (4.8, 7.5) — installable bundles of skills + connectors + commands + configuration. A plugin is a PACKAGED SOLUTION for a specific workflow or profession — 'the accounting-firm AI plugin' bundling the receipt skill, the report format, the reconciliation checklist, the relevant connectors. You sell a complete, installable capability set. MCP servers (8.4) — the integration products. If a tool or system lacks a good MCP server (8.3's gap), BUILD and SELL one. 'An MCP server that connects [popular niche tool] to the AI ecosystem' is a product whose buyers are everyone who uses that tool and wants AI integration. Your 8.4 build skill becomes a product. The distribution advantage: these sell through the ECOSYSTEM's channels — marketplaces, registries (8.3), directories — which provide built-in distribution you don't have to create. A skill/plugin/server in a marketplace gets discovered by people searching for exactly that capability. The ecosystem is your distribution. The 'productize the repeated service' path: if you've built the same skill/integration for multiple service clients (Track 11), packaging it as a sellable product means building once, selling many — the service you've done repeatedly becomes a product others install. Proven demand, built asset, ecosystem distribution.

Why these are attractive product forms

Faster to build than full SaaS: a skill or MCP server is a focused artifact (Tracks 4.8, 8.4), not a full application with accounts, billing, hosting, and UI. You can build and ship one in days, not weeks/months. Lower economics risk: often sold as a one-time purchase or included in a subscription, with the buyer bringing their OWN AI usage (like AI-powered artifacts, 4.5 — their account, their tokens) — so YOU don't carry the per-user API cost that complicates SaaS economics (12.2). This sidesteps the unit-economics trap. Encoded expertise scales: your judgment, encoded once in a skill, sells to everyone who needs that expertise — the leverage of a product without the full build. It's the purest 'serialize your judgment and sell it repeatedly' form. Ecosystem-native distribution: the marketplaces and registries surface your product to people searching for exactly it — distribution you'd struggle to build for a standalone SaaS. The honest caveat: the market for these is younger and smaller than full-product markets, pricing conventions are still forming, and distribution channels are maturing — so it's an emerging opportunity (upside: less competition; downside: less established). A realistic path: use these as one product form among your options, often alongside services (Track 11) and possibly a full product (12.1-12.3).

Recap

Beyond full SaaS, sell packaged EXPERTISE — Skills (encoded scarce judgment), plugins (bundled solutions), and MCP servers (integration products, 8.4) — which are faster to build, lower economic risk (buyers often bring their own AI usage, sidestepping the unit-economics trap), and ecosystem-distributed; the strongest come from services you've delivered repeatedly, and all three product forms (service, full product, packaged expertise) can be built from one niche expertise to serve different segments.
