3.1 · Prompt Chaining: Divide and Conquer

One prompt asking for five things gets five mediocre things. Chains get five excellent things — and they're the seed of every agent you'll ever build.

In one breath: Prompt chaining means decomposing a big job into focused steps — each prompt does one thing well and feeds the next — trading one blurry mega-prompt for a pipeline of sharp ones.

Why chains beat mega-prompts

Three compounding reasons: Attention is a budget. A prompt juggling five goals splits focus five ways (1.2's physics). Each chain step gets Claude's entire capability pointed at one target. Errors stop propagating silently. In a mega-prompt, a bad assumption in the «research» flows invisibly into the «report». In a chain, YOU inspect between steps — the checkpoint is the point. One weak link gets fixed without redoing the rest. Steps become reusable parts. A great «summarise interviews» step gets reused in ten future chains (and becomes a template in 3.5). Mega-prompts are monoliths; chains are Lego. The professional smell test: if your prompt contains two unrelated verbs, it wants to be two prompts. «Analyse and rewrite» — chain it. «Extract, then compare, then recommend» — three links.

Designing a chain

Work backwards from the deliverable: 1. Name the final artifact (2.4 shape-first thinking): «a 2-page brief recommending one vendor, with a comparison table.» 2. Ask what that step needs as input — a filled comparison table → which needs per-vendor extractions → which needs the raw documents. Your chain just designed itself in reverse: extract (×3 vendors) → compare → recommend. 3. Define each link's output format so it snaps into the next link's input (2.4's tags shine here — a <table> out of step 2 is pasted into step 3's <table> slot). 4. Place your checkpoints. Between which links will you actually look? Minimum: after the extraction (facts checkable against source — 1.6) and before anything client-facing. Where chains live: in chat, a chain is just consecutive messages — or separate chats when steps deserve clean contexts (a critic step ALWAYS deserves a fresh chat — 1.6's fresh-eyes principle). Later, the same designs run automatically: Cowork executes multi-step plans (Track 5), and the API version is literally called workflow chaining (Track 10). Same skill, rising altitude.

Three chain shapes to recognise

The refinery (linear): raw → structured → analysed → drafted → polished. Most documents want this. The map-reduce: same prompt over many items (summarise each of 30 reviews) → then one prompt over the results (find patterns across summaries). This is how long work beats context limits — each item gets a fresh, focused pass. The generator–critic: produce in one step, attack in another (fresh context), revise in a third. You met the idea in 1.6; as a chain it becomes standard practice for anything high-stakes.

Recap

Two unrelated verbs = two prompts: decompose backwards from the deliverable, design snap-together formats, stand at checkpoints after facts and before exits — refinery, map-reduce, generator–critic cover almost everything.
